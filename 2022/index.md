## Workshop Overview

The objective of this workshop is to provide the opportunity to share practices and experiences about high-performance Arm computing systems and their performance and applications.

The world's fastest Japanese supercomputer Fugaku used Fujitsu A64FX Arm-based manycore processors, and Astra at Sandia National Laboratories was the first large-scale Arm-based supercomputer using Marvell ThunderX2. The last few years have seen an explosion of 64-bit Arm-based processors targeted toward server and infrastructure workloads, often with a specialization toward domains such as HPC, cloud, and machine learning. Arm Neoverse N1 became the foundation for processors such as Amazon Graviton2 and Ampere Altra, with the EPI project incorporating the successor design into SiPearl Rhea. NVIDIA Grace had also been announced for future supercomputing deployments, and GPU-integrated Arm clusters were emerging as a category of HPC clusters.

In this workshop, papers were invited on the practice and experience of Arm-based high-performance computing systems and on optimization and performance analysis of high-performance workloads for this generation of Arm-based processors.

## Topics

Topics of interest included:

- HPC Applications on Arm-based systems
- Performance Analysis, Performance Modeling & Measurement of Arm-based systems
- SVE Vectorisation analysis
- Programming Models & Systems Software related to Arm-based systems
- Networking and accelerators such as GPUs for Arm-based systems
- Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning using Arm-based systems
- Emerging Technologies of Arm-based systems
